### Feelings, broadcast

Radio doesn't know or care what it's carrying — a number, a word, a secret
handshake code. Here it carries a feeling: pressing B sends a single word
("happy", "calm", "excited"...) into the air, and any duck listening on the
same channel picks it up and mirrors it back as its own face and colour.

### Why this is harder than it sounds

Telling a friend how you feel out loud is easy when you're standing next to
them. Over distance, without talking, you need a **shared code** both sides
already agree on — that's exactly what the `FEELINGS` list is: a vocabulary
two ducks both understand, so a single word like `"calm"` reliably becomes
the same face and the same colour on both ends.

> Real satellites and radios work the same way — a shared protocol both
> sides trust, so a burst of static becomes a meaningful message.
